From the notebook

Short entries, in the order they happened.

Day 1

The nets at dusk

We reached Fort Kochi after dark and walked straight to the water. The fishermen were hauling the nets by hand, slowly, as they have for centuries. A man selling roasted peanuts told us the best light is just before the lamps come on.

Day 3

Tea, and a wrong turn

The bus to Top Station was late, so we walked part of it through the estates. Pickers in plastic aprons waved from the slopes. Our wrong turn ended at a tiny shop selling tea so fresh it was still warm to smell.

Day 5

Inside the spice farm

Cardamom grows on the ground, not on trees, which surprised everyone. The farmer crushed a pod in my palm and told me to keep it for the road. It is still in my bag.

Day 6

Eleven hours on a houseboat

No signal, no schedule. Kingfishers, coconut palms, kids waving from canoes on the way to school. Dinner was karimeen fried in a pan on the back deck while the sun went down behind the paddy fields.

Day 8

The last sunset

Varkala was the quietest stop. We sat on the cliff path, shared a coconut and said very little. The sea was loud enough for all of us.

Before you go

What we would tell a friend.

Best time

October to March is dry and cool. June to August is lush and wet, and the houseboats get cheaper.

Getting around

Trains link Kochi, Alleppey and Varkala. Take a taxi or a state bus for the hill sections.

What to pack

A light rain jacket, a warm layer for Munnar, mosquito repellent and a small day bag.

Book ahead

Houseboats and Periyar boat rides sell out in peak season. Reserve both before you arrive.
